# Coffeehouses and science: access to knowledge in historical London

## Overview

- **What it is**: Economic-history research on coffeehouse openings and applied science.
- **What it says**: A staggered parish study finds increases in practitioners and instrument shops.
- **Caveat**: Nonrandom entry, surviving records and model assumptions constrain causal and modern interpretations.

## Practical reading (6 lines)

- The question is whether venues broaden access to scientific exchange.
- The design compares opening dates across 162 London parishes.
- Practitioners and instrument shops rise; mechanisms suggest links between craft and theory.
- The headline 31% is a calibrated counterfactual share of scientist-decades, not a direct count or growth rate.
- Proposed use: test whether a study group connects people with complementary skills.
- This does not show that coffee consumption, chat volume or AI-agent count causes innovation.

## Method, evidence and limitations

Identification assumes no anticipation and parallel untreated trends. Placebos and spillover checks address alternatives. European-city evidence is explicitly conditional correlation; it also calibrates the aggregate model. Elite ties are proxied by surname representation, not individual attendance.
