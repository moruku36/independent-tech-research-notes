# Slack emoji as a satirical measure of company culture

## Overview

- Liam Galvin’s satire on reducing company culture to a Slack workspace statistic.
- The essay proposes dividing the number of custom emoji by the square root of workspace users and counting the share of users who have uploaded any.
- The formula and cutoffs are humorous proposals without validation against company culture.

## Six practical takeaways

- The emoji coefficient is custom emoji divided by the square root of the workspace user count.
- The square-root adjustment reflects the author's premise that emoji are shared, so inventory need not grow linearly with users.
- The participation measure counts the share of users who have uploaded an emoji. It illustrates that total volume can hide contributor concentration.
- The article jokes that mass uploads inflate the numerator, while a smaller workspace inflates the score if its emoji inventory stays fixed.
- Upload activity and workspace size are observable inputs. The source gives no evidence that its thresholds measure the underlying culture.
- Proposed use. Try a synthetic example to separate changes in the numerator, denominator and participation before interpreting a proxy metric.

## Publication and review scope

Liam Galvin, “Company Culture = Slack Emoji ÷ √Headcount.” Reviewed the published article on November 28, 2026 JST. No workspace data was accessed.

## Evidence and limitations

- The essay is written as satire. Its thresholds and examples illustrate potential readings but are not calibrated to empirical outcomes.
- The numerator measures inventory and the participation measure reflects upload behavior. A workspace's emoji library can accumulate over time, and its number of users can change independently.
- Holding the numerator fixed makes a smaller denominator raise the score by construction. Observing participation could expose a concentration of contributors, but the article does not establish construct validity.

## Application and low-cost check

Proposed and untried. Compare fictional workspaces with the same emoji count and different numbers of users or contributors. Explain what changed in the arithmetic and what remains unknown about the team.
