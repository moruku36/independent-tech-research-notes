# Meta’s Open Source AI for Science Summit: resources and evidence

## Overview

- **What it is** Meta FAIR’s recap of a two-day science summit held in October 2026.
- **What it says** The event highlighted shared research resources, classroom tools, and machine-checked mathematics.
- **Caveat** The recap combines targets, demonstrations, and releases.

## Practical reading (six lines)

- The Digital Brain Project raised its data collection target from 15,000 to 25,000 hours.
- The summit featured UMA Nitro, described as a faster version of the atomistic simulation model.
- The article describes classroom availability for UMA Playground and planned integration into RealChem.
- Meta describes ATLAS as formalizing 26 open-access textbooks and more than 45,000 definitions and results.
- Lean can check a proof against its formal statement. Researchers still decide whether that statement expresses the intended claim.
- For a small review, trace one formal result to the text it represents and record assumptions.

## Source and reading scope

Meta Newsroom, November 20, 2026. Checked the organizer’s recap and linked ATLAS repository landing page on November 21, 2026 JST.

## Evidence and limitations

The data-hours figure is a target. The ATLAS counts are reported by Meta, and this reading only confirmed the linked repository. Project outcomes, textbook coverage, and classroom effectiveness were not independently verified.

## Application and free check

Proposed and untried. Pick one elementary theorem and record the source statement, the formal version, and any differences in hypotheses. A local Lean check could test the formal proof if the required environment is already available.
