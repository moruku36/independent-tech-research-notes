# Curiosity, metacognition and learning in the age of AI

## Overview
- **What it is:** A research-synthesis and conceptual-framework chapter on curiosity, metacognition, education and generative AI.
- **What it argues:** Monitoring knowledge gaps and regulating exploration support curiosity-driven learning.
- **Caveat:** It synthesizes prior work and proposes hypotheses; it is not one new intervention experiment.

## Source and scope
Chloé Desvaux, Rania Abdelghani, Pierre-Yves Oudeyer and Hélène Sauzéon, [Curiosity and Metacognition: Towards a Unified Framework for Learning and Education in the Age of AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25648v1), arXiv:2604.25648v1, 28 April 2026. Selected primary HTML sections on the abstract, interventions and AI opportunities/risks checked 2 November 2026 JST. Individual cited studies and PDF were not independently checked; no reproduction.

## Question and synthesis
Metacognition is monitoring one's own understanding and regulating learning. The authors synthesize behavioral, computational and psychoeducational literature into a framework linking curiosity to self-regulated exploration. They review school interventions and discuss implications of generative AI.

## Findings and limits
The chapter describes promising but heterogeneous intervention results, with limited evidence of sustained effects in natural settings or transfer to other domains. Authors argue that passive consumption of fluent AI answers risks an illusion of understanding and loss of learner agency, while hints and questions could scaffold inquiry. Transfer from prompt training to general questioning is explicitly a hypothesis, not an outcome established by this chapter.

## Proposed applications
When learning cloud architecture, write an initial hypothesis and knowledge gaps; ask AI for counterexamples and further questions; check primary sources and explain the result independently. This is a proposal, not evidence that school interventions work in every workplace. Cited studies and intervention effects were not independently validated.
