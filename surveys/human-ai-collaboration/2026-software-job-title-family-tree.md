# Software job titles: a practitioner’s family tree

## Overview

- **What it is**: A Claude-assisted visualization of 104 software job titles in seven families.
- **What it says**: The author interprets titles as splitting, recombining and being reused.
- **Caveat**: Dates are estimates of common usage, not a sourced chronology or hiring dataset.

## Source and reading scope

- Ian Miell (imiell), *A Family Tree of Software Job Titles*, 2026. URL dated November 3; the page displays both November 15 and November 3 without an unambiguous date label.

## Practical reading

- The question is how software roles acquire and exchange their names.
- The author arranges titles as an editable family tree.
- It suggests splitting and recombination; historical dates remain estimates.
- Proposed use: map responsibilities and skills before assigning team titles.
- Do the same for agent roles, linking each to permissions and acceptance evidence.
- The chart does not measure hiring demand or predict career growth.

## Method, evidence and limitations

The graph uses five-year rows, solid ancestry arrows and dashed influence/career links. Graphviz and JSON make the representation editable. Its relationships are the author's interpretation.
