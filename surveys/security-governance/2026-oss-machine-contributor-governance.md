# Regulating the Machine Contributor: Governance and Policy Alignment in Open Source

## Overview
- **What it is:** A preprint comparing open-source AI contribution policies.
- **What it says:** Six dimensions expose differences and gaps in contribution governance.
- **Caveat:** Six organizations' public documents are a snapshot through 7 May 2026; the score is the authors' instrument.

## Source and scope
Jassem Manita and Aziz Amari, [arXiv:2606.14594v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14594v1), 12 June 2026. Selected primary HTML sections checked on 1 November 2026 JST. Underlying policies and incidents were not independently cross-checked; PDF, code and reproduction not checked.

## Question and method
Autonomous agents can submit contributions into governance processes designed around accountable human contributors. The authors code public policies from SymPy, LLVM, matplotlib, OpenInfra, Apache Software Foundation and Linux Foundation across disclosure, responsibility, human oversight, licensing, enforcement and maintainer workload. They trace policy formation in SymPy and LLVM and map public incidents and governance frameworks to these dimensions.

## Findings and interpretation
The authors find differences in disclosure, human answerability and licensing emphasis. They report that none of the six policies provides a structural mechanism to protect reviewer capacity against submission volume. Their summed ordinal score measures policy provisions under their rubric, not actual safety or prevention. Incident mapping is a conceptual stress test, not evidence that higher scores would have prevented harm. Regulatory alignment is the authors' analysis, not a legal determination.

## Proposed applications
Before an AI team contributes externally, check the project's current rules and define disclosure, a responsible human, review and understanding of generated changes, provenance and licensing. For a repository one manages, consider rate limits, triage and maintainer workload. These are proposals, not measured effects of this study.

## Limits and next checks
The source corpus stops at 7 May 2026; policies can change. Contributor-level rules and foundation-level guidance differ in scope, and private deliberations are unavailable. Independent coding and replication remain necessary; implementation and adoption have not been performed.
