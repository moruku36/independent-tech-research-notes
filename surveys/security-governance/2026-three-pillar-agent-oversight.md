# Transparency, accountability and trustworthy AI agents

## Overview
- **What it is:** A preprint proposing a conceptual and operational safety framework for AI agents.
- **What it argues:** Increase autonomy through progressive validation, supported by transparency, accountability and human collaboration.
- **Caveat:** The paper proposes a framework; it does not establish its effects in a controlled comparative experiment.

## Question and proposal
Agents need visible actions, attributable decisions and human oversight as their authority grows. The authors draw an analogy to staged development of autonomous driving and propose progressing from limited assistance through collaboration to supervised autonomy. Their pillars are transparency across the lifecycle, accountability for decisions, and trustworthiness through human collaboration and fallback safeguards. This is a proposal, not evidence that a staged process guarantees safety.

## Proposed applications
For cloud operations, start with read-only investigation, then human-approved change proposals. Define permissions, records, stop criteria, owner and rollback before expanding authority; review evidence at each stage. These are proposals, not demonstrated benefits in the user's environment.

## Limits
The framework does not experimentally establish quantitative safety rates, precise stage-transition thresholds or human-oversight effectiveness. A recorded explanation is not proof of the model's actual decision process. No independent verification, implementation or reproduction.
